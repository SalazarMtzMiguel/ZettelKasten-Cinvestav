\subsection{Future Work}

The analysis of the current literature indicates that automated software design has achieved significant theoretical progress, evolving from traditional self-adaptive approaches toward paradigms of self-evolution and generative design. Recent advances integrating metaheuristics, computational reflection architectures, and multi-agent systems supported by large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated the potential to enable software systems to reason about their own structure and generate solutions for specific design tasks. However, several critical gaps still hinder the consolidation of this research area and its adoption in industrial contexts. A primary limitation is the fragmented treatment of the different phases of software design, which are typically addressed in isolation, leading to approaches that optimize architectural structures, components, or code independently rather than providing an integrated end-to-end design workflow. Additionally, a considerable portion of the proposed methods remains at a conceptual level, lacking large-scale empirical validation using real-world industrial repositories. Consequently, future research should focus on integrating these isolated phases into a continuous and implementable design workflow. In particular, promising directions include the development of mechanisms for extracting, structuring, and refining domain knowledge—such as through ontologies or opportunistic learning techniques—to enable artificial intelligence systems to more accurately select appropriate architectural styles and design patterns for specific problems. Furthermore, exploring context-aware multi-agent mechanisms and promoting synergistic collaboration between artificial intelligence and human engineers will be essential to transition automated self-design from a predominantly theoretical concept to a practical and reliable tool for real-world software engineering.
